## 1. The problem, in the user's words

"MettleUp's privacy notice, websites and screens tell me where my data goes. Some of those sentences are not true."

Examples, each checked against the page that makes the claim today:
- Since 25 Nov, a proven phone number creates a MettleUp account that keeps workouts, routines and body weight
  (migration 0099, live). The notice (EN and HI) still says "stays on your phone, unless you sign in with Google";
  the delete-account page says "You only have an account if you tapped Continue with Google", and its only
  route without the app is an e-mail "from the Google address you signed in with": a phone-only member cannot
  use it. Play links to this page as the deletion page.
- The notice says "Your progress photos never leave your phone". With "Include photos in my backup" on, Android's
  backup copies the photo folder to the member's Google account (backup-rules plugin).
- The safekeep card and R-630's new under-18 sheet say "your workouts are only on this phone". Android's backup
  can copy the workout database too, and the app cannot read whether that backup is on.
- A gym owner's spoken AI Assistant request can go to Sarvam (or, when Sarvam's key is not set, to Groq in the
  USA). Neither the notice nor the data agreement names them (S1). The agreement promises to tell owners before a
  new company sees their records; Claude (Assistant), Groq, Sarvam and Google (HQ English helper) are missing.
- The owner website says "Your member data is stored in India" and gyms were given a copy-paste notice "servers
  in Mumbai, India". The nightly copy sits in Cloudflare storage that is spread worldwide. (The privacy notice
  itself already names the Cloudflare nightly copy.)
- The owner website says MettleUp staff "cannot open your member list unless you let them in for 1 hour". HQ has
  an access button with the reasons emergency, "owner locked out" and "other"; and the nightly copy can be
  opened by anyone with the Cloudflare login, with no record.
- Trainer sharing went from 30 workouts over 3 months to up to 400 over 12 months on 14 Nov, on by default, with
  no in-app message, though the notice and terms promise "Before a change that affects you, we tell you in the app".
- Phone sign-in and Find my gym say "we never message you"; MettleUp's WhatsApp number writes back after the check.
- The websites say they send nothing out; every page (and the admin app, gym pages, owner inbox) loads its
  letters (fonts) from Google.
- Door punches from entry machines are kept for ever and not named.
- The store listing and member website still promise "Back up to Google Drive"; the app no longer offers it.
- The notice says "If your data leaks, we tell you"; the leak plan leaves members to the gym and HQ cannot reach them.
- The data agreement says a leaving gym's payments are deleted within 30 days; the web server's own payment
  records (one row per payment request) have no expiry and no delete.
- 31 privacy promises on screens are true today, but no test would notice when a change breaks them.

The cause is one thing: every privacy sentence is typed by hand, in many files, and nothing compares it with
what the code really sends and keeps.

## 3. What users see after the change

### 3a. Everyone
- The notice (EN and HI), the data agreement, the delete-account page, the websites and the store listing say
  what really happens, built from the list (tables of "who gets it" and "how long" are generated; prose
  sentences carry a marker and are checked).
- Every outside company that gets data is named where it applies, with what and how long, or the flow is
  removed (decisions D1-D6).
- "Only on this phone" is never said of anything Android's backup may copy. New wording (in W): "on this phone,
  and in your phone's own Google backup if that is on in Android settings". Same rule for photos.
- A phone-number account is described everywhere a Google account is, and the delete-account page offers a
  delete route by number (D11).

### 3b. Members: the one-time change message
- One mechanism, shared with R-630: the app reads the current notice version from the server (not from the app
  build), compares it with the version this member last saw, and:
  - when the change needs a new tick (R-630's member version), shows R-630's re-ask sheet with a "What changed"
    list. That sheet IS the change message; no second line for the same change;
  - otherwise shows one dismissible line at the top of Home: "We updated the privacy notice. What changed" with a
    link; dismissed = never shown again for that version on that phone; also marked seen when the re-ask sheet
    was answered.
- A fresh install stores the current version as seen: it never says "we updated".
- Trainer sharing (D8): current members get the line plus a "Change what my trainer sees" button that opens the
  sharing switch.
- Old app versions still read the web notice; their old screen sentences stay until they update (the notice is
  the binding text). R-631's release ships through the normal Play path; this limit is written in the release note.

### 3c. Gym owners
- The data agreement gets one new version, shared with R-630's owner version (one re-accept, never two in a
  week): R-630 and R-631 slice B release together, or R-631 raises the version R-630 set. Owners see the
  existing "accept again" step, plus the change in What's new. The agreement lists every company that sees gym
  records (Claude, Groq, Sarvam, Google, Cloudflare, Supabase, payment companies as live).
- Owners whose gym copied the "servers in Mumbai" notice get one What's new item with the new text to replace it.
- If D7 = A: owners are told in What's new before door punches older than 13 months are deleted, and gym reports
  say "door entries: last 13 months".

### 3d. Must NOT see
- A privacy sentence in the repo (screens, pages, help articles, listing draft, Hindi included) that a code change
  has made false: CI fails first. (The live Play listing and Data safety form are not in the repo: a release step
  ticks them against the generated file, 9g.)
- A company that receives data but is not in the notice and agreement.
- "Stays on this phone" for data a switch the member turned on sends, or that Android's backup may copy.
- The wrong variant of a sentence, or one variant flashing into another, while the member's state is still
  loading: such sentences show the cautious variant until the state is read.
- An English sentence fixed while its Hindi twin stays false.
- A notice change no member or owner is told about; a new member seeing "We updated"; the line coming back after
  it was dismissed; both R-630's sheet and this line for the same change; owners asked to re-accept twice in a week.
- A flow removed without warning: lost door history (D7), spoken requests gone (D1 B), trainers losing months (D8
  B), websites looking different after fonts move (D2 A: same fonts, same look, Hindi font included; checked by
  the website screenshot test).
- Any new wording live before his yes (W).

## 8. Risks and the pre-mortem
- The list drifts: checked from both sides; a new host, table, plugin or backup rule fails CI until listed.
- The allow-list becomes the new hand-typed drift: CI fails if it grows; each row has an end date; no S1 row allowed.
- Words state unchecked facts: each outside-company row has a source link read before the words are written (D1, D3).
- Two cards change the notice: one notice version and one owner version, shared with R-630 (3b, 3c).
- Words break the phone UAT flows: the qa folders are in the boundary and updated with the words.
- A switched-off feature (AI coach) is turned on later: its rows carry the switch; CI fails when a switch is on and
  its flow is not in the notice.
- Too big: four slices, each shippable alone; A and C need no new words live.

## Glossary
- Data flow: one kind of data going from one place to another, with who sees it and how long it is kept.
- Allow-list: the known gaps the new check accepts today, each with an end date; it may only shrink.
- Promise registry: one file per app holding every privacy sentence a screen may show, with what it depends on.
- Notice version: the "Last updated" date of the privacy notice, served by the server as one value the apps read.
- Lane Full, goldfish round, G0-G7, [F]: the No Gaps framework (skill change-360): Full = design note, three fresh
  reviewers, his yes; G0-G7 = family, design, review, tests first, build, fresh review, proof, delivered.
- S1-S4: audit severity, S1 worst. Watch screen log: the watch's opt-in diagnostics (apps/mobile/src/lib/watchDiag.ts).
- member-words: the skill that writes and checks words users read.
